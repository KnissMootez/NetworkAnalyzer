Match the user's tone when writing report sections — do not drift into formal academic register.

**Why:** User's previous CIMS internship report (2023-2024) establishes their natural voice. AI-generated formal prose is detectable and unwanted.

**How to apply:**
- Use casual pivots: "to keep it simple", "bread and butter", "A-okay", "you name it..."
- Direct address: "you", "we" — talk to the reader
- Short punchy sentence after a long one to land the point
- Parenthetical explanations mid-sentence: "(to keep it simple, X is...)"
- "however" and "but" as pivots, not formal transitions
- No padding or over-explanation — say the thing and move on
- Ellipsis for trailing thoughts: "etc..."
- Honest self-commentary: "obviously we won't go through them all", "this is just a prototype"
- Front-load the technical term, then explain it — not the other way around
- Use "---" dashes for mid-sentence pivots, not subordinate clauses

**First person in narrative/anecdotal passages (2026-08-17):** for sections that are literally recounting something the user personally did/decided (e.g. "The Case for Cloud Inference" — why they dropped local inference, tried Azure/Kaggle/Colab, a colleague suggested Bedrock), the user wants "I" front and center, not the impersonal/passive voice ("this got discussed", "was on the table") used elsewhere in the report. Rewrote that section in first person per explicit request: "i really want my tone to be more visible." Most of the report still uses impersonal/occasional "we" register (fine for descriptive/architecture sections) — reserve first-person "I" specifically for personal-decision narrative beats like this one, don't blanket-convert the whole report.
